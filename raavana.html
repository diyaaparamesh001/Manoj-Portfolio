Raavana

By Manoj Ashodia · Lanka, the Golden City

Pouring out all his might and creativity on the gigantic veena resting on his lap, the Demon King drove the entire palace numb and silent as if the world had come to a halt, basking in the glory of the one true musical God at his terrifying best. Nobody moved an inch, and not because they were following a protocol. No one wanted to abandon the chance to relish this exquisite sound emanating out of the golden palace standing tall right into the centre of the golden city of Lanka. The purple walls of the music room were supposed to be soundproof, as the engineers had declared, but then, they had never heard the Lankesh performing music.

With his mountainous physique, he was seated on a velvet mat with the enormous instrument on his lap, and his fingers moved restlessly on the strings, bleeding, creating the kind of heavenly music only the one truly blessed by Saraswati could. This utter passion for melody either results from extreme pain or extreme joy, as any real musician can testify.

While she herself was hypnotized by it, Mandodari, his beautiful wife, lying in her bedroom a few paces away waiting for him, knew what it was all about. The pain she was feeling, of unrequited love, resonated with the pain in her husband's heart, channelling through a veena as if Raavan understood her heartache and was striving to express it through the music. But then she knew that both their aches told two different stories and arose from two very different feelings. She felt it for her husband, and he, for the lonely another man's woman sitting in Ashok Vatika.

It was the first time in months that he had been to the music room alone and that too deep into the night. Usually, he entered the room with 10–12 of his closest art patrons from around the empire, to exhibit his skills and learn new things; that was if they, in fact, could teach him anything. When it came to music, Raavan was no narcissist.

This late-night session meant that something else was bothering him as well, something huge, something not beyond Mandodari's unmaligned sensibilities could conjure up. Her innocent mind of a love-hungry wife wished that the remorse he was feeling was due to his inability to love Mandodari as she deserved. Or perhaps, his agony was triggered by the realization that he had caused immense heartache to Ram, the abducted woman's husband. Or perhaps it was a brutal combination of the three, rendering the greatest King in all three worlds sleepless.

Mandodari knew her husband, and she was aware that in all the years, she had not seen him being unjust, and perhaps the thoughts of his recent erratic actions were eating him up.

She sighed and kept her gaze locked on the golden door of the bedroom, waiting for her husband to emerge from the gigantic door. As much as she wanted the angelic music to resume, she was aching to hear the thumping footsteps of her beloved husband approaching her more than anything else.

A loud string break sound marked the end of the glorious session. The golden Veena was drenched in red-hot half-Brahmin, half-Raakshasa blood, and several strings on the instrument had broken irreparably. It upset Raavan as a dozen engineers dashed towards the music room to comprehend the damage and offer suggestions on whether to replace or repair it. But Raavan's anguish knew no borders that night. He threw the engineers out, holding his beloved instrument as if it were his wounded child. The possessor of almost every material thing in the universe was burning inside; what was it that he couldn't get? It was not about his favourite veena, for sure! Then what was it?

What else, but the ethereal beauty that was not a material possession, a woman named Sita, sobbing silently in the garden a few meters away, terrified of the music which seemed more like a battle cry to her, a melody of horror. And then, her heart ached too, for her husband Ram, who was waiting across the ocean searching for her, preparing to embark on a military campaign to rescue her at the first sign of her whereabouts. It had been years since Raavan abducted Sita, and her hopes were sinking with every passing day.

Mandodari, Raavan's wife, was by no means less beautiful than Sita. Obviously, it couldn't be just about the looks. For all her demonic genes, Mandodari was sympathetic to Sita, for she had been snatched away from her lover, her husband, who was dying to see her. Besides, no woman, whether she be a human or a demon queen, can bear the pain of her husband ruining his sleep over some other woman. Mandodari was a woman strong of character and loved her husband more than anything and anyone else in the entire universe. Despite his characteristic lapses, she always knew there was a whole other side to her husband that was generally hidden from plain sight — crept inside silently, stripped of any desire to be measured by the very human perceptions of morality and immorality. Raavan was not a human; hence, the concept of principles was alien to him. Garbed in arrogance, his greatness was always doomed to be feared, not loved or respected.

Geniuses are more often than not difficult to understand, and abhorred by the majority. Outside this little Southern island of Lanka, Raavan was just a demon king who 'deserved' to be destroyed.

At dawn, he finally opened the shiny entrance of his sleep chamber and stood by his bed, staring at Mandodari. Faintly snoring, she had decided to shut her eyes for a moment but ended up launching herself into a deep sleep. Her milky white skin, paired with a beautiful red saree, with absolutely no ornamentation or makeup, she was as gorgeous a woman any man can hope to have — and yet, Raavan's heart was stuck on that helpless damsel who was crying her eyes out all day all night, possibly the only woman in the entire creation who didn't have any kind of desire for this man, or perhaps the first he'd met.

As he sat down on the corner of the bed, his wife opened her eyes, and with beautifully adorned eyelashes, paired with an angelic smile, she glanced at him. Raavan's heart stopped for a moment at the purity of her countenance. She was a heavenly beauty. Indeed, he was feeling guilty for destroying the trust this woman invested in him since the day they got tied together in the marital bond. What good is that guilt if it doesn't constrain him to change his ways? Eternal shame was there in his eyes, and he couldn't hold eye contact for long. He looked away and lay down on the bed.

"You must be tired. You should sleep a little, my love," she uttered while shifting her petite body to her left. The bed was massive; it could've easily swallowed 10 people, and yet, given Raavan's massive build, they had to be as close as they physically could every time they lay together in the bed. Raavan removed his chest cloth and turned towards her. Just as his blood-stained fingers moved up to touch Mandodari's face, her eyes caught hold of the blood. She didn't panic; she knew what Raavan needed at that moment, and it wasn't the ointment made of herbs, but of love — the eternal passionate love, which had kept Raavan bound to her for many years.

She softly kissed the index finger of his left hand, and slowly swirled her tongue around it, swallowing every single drop of blood. She had her eyes closed, and when she heard soft snores, she knew he had slept. She looked up to confirm; she was right, he was dozing, leaving her thirst for lovemaking unquenched, yet again. She put her head on his heaving chest and heard his heart thumping.

He was a muscular, powerful man with a Godly aura and incomprehensible intelligence, and no woman could keep her eyes off him, whether a human or a demon. The maidservants, nymphs, and the devotees residing in the palace had all tried to lure him into the beds, both men and women, to savour making love to the most perfect man there was. But to their dismay, he remained loyal to his wife unwaveringly. He didn't desire sex; he considered himself to be way too enlightened to pursue sensory pleasures. But that was only until the moment his eyes met Sita, coming out of the small hut while her husband and brother-in-law were out hunting. He had heard about the Great Beauty from his sister Shoorpanakha, but nothing she said to describe her did justice to the feeling of looking at Sita for the first time. But every beautiful thing in the world comes with a cost. Right from the moment he laid his eyes upon her, he had the intuition that it was a doomed affair from the very beginning. Abducting her was an easy process; actually transporting her from that wilderness across the sea to his golden city of Lanka was the real task. She didn't give up easily. She cursed, she fought, she bit him, and there were a couple of instances where he thought about throwing her into the ocean as they flew their way above it. Yes, there were moments. But that would have been easy.

Killing a woman is one of the easiest things to do. But winning her over is not.

The thing is, when you really want a woman, you start thinking from your heart; your rationality goes for a toss, and when it comes to that, even the Gods among men like Raavan fall into the same trap. That was what had happened to Raavan. He had lost himself the moment he laid his eyes on her the very first time, draped in an orange saree, when she was awaiting her husband in that dangerous Panchavati forest all alone. He picked the right time to approach her, camouflaged as a sage, and made his move, swept her off her feet in an instant, and before she came to consciousness, they were flying through the sky in the Pushpak Viman. While he was flying over the ocean, he sensed his approaching end, for what he had just done was a vile thing that he himself didn't know he was capable of pulling off. The rumours were that her husband was no simple man, but a reincarnation of Lord Vishnu himself. But now, it was too late.

"You slept well?" Raavan asked his wife as he woke up a few hours later, sounding guilty and tired.

"I liked the music; which raga was it?" Mandodari smiled, concealing her tears.

Raavan scrutinised her gorgeous face, put his lips on her forehead and touched her eyelashes with his massive blood-soaked fingers. The blood had dried up.

"You should sleep, my love. It's about time," he whispered, knowing very well that she hadn't slept well.

Mandodari sighed. And closed her eyes as sleep dawned upon her in an instant.

The afternoon was pretty much routine. He walked up to Sita, who was sitting under a mammoth Neem tree, enveloped in longing and melancholy, exactly where he had left her the last day. She hadn't moved an inch. He threw an infuriating look at her keeper, Suman, a demon slave. She gasped. Raavan looked around; not a sign of air, not a leaf moved on any of the trees. So majestic was Raavan's aura that the world went silent whenever he approached the garden full of all sorts of plants, medicinal herbs, and trees. His ominous presence was unwelcoming even in his own garden. There was no sound at all, the world had become a massive vacuum. There was no usual chirping of birds on the trees, no chatter from across the walls of the garden which was adjacent to the market. As if the world stood still in his presence. He graced the Ashok Vatika every day with his intimidating visit, and the traditional pattern of time and space changed its ways. As his angry stare pierced Suman, her breath jammed in her throat.

"I… I tried, my lord. She isn't ready yet… to eat," she stuttered.

Raavan turned his gaze towards Sita, shedding tears. For a brief moment, he felt a wave of melancholy washing over him. The emotions took over, yet again. He had the choice of making that one moment linger around till eternity, holding on to that one emotion, could've kept feeling bad for her, and he knew that eventually, a deep voice inside him would compel him to return her back to where she really belonged, to Ram. But he didn't do it and pushed himself back again, to the present — the trait all the great kings must possess, and he surely was one!

"The problem is Sita, you aren't a thinking woman. If you were, what better choice could the Gods offer you, other than me? I can feel your heart, and I know I am still far away from having a place there. But I must tell you, I am not giving up. I have fallen in love with you, and nothing's going to change that. As for you, I don't know what you desire; a woman's heart is a complex mystery. A maze of uncertainties; nobody knows what lies at the very next turn. I could have read your face, only if my brain let me, as whenever my eyes catch sight of you, I can't think of anything else, other than the fact that I am hopelessly in love with you." He breathed deeply and then continued. "I am going to leave you alone now. I hope tomorrow is a better day for your understanding of how things are. And… please… eat something! Your starvation isn't going to help your husband find you!"

Sita looked up into his eyes. They were calm, as calm as the surroundings. Despite the emotional despair Raavan must have been going through, he was always strangely calm and composed, his deep baritone always so firm. He stared into Sita's sleepless eyes. They had lost their charm since he first saw them years back when he abducted her. He smiled a grim smile and left; his heart would have melted right then and there. He couldn't stand the sight of Sita's bloodshot, swollen eyes any longer; the image would linger in his head for days. Even during the inevitable moment when he'd be face-to-face with Ram on the battlefield. And his favourite veena would take weeks to get fixed before he could lose himself in his music and forget all about Sita's dovey eyes full of pain and terror. The outlook didn't seem too favourable for Raavan.

"He plays amazing veena, right?" Suman came rushing back to Sita as Raavan disappeared into his palace and wiped away her tears. "He does that whenever he is sad, or very happy. And trust me, when he is sad, that is no good."

"Yes, he plays well, and I've heard about his skills. Back in Janakpur, my paternal home, I had practised veena for so long. I myself know my way around it. My teacher Sage Atharvadhwani referred to Raavan's compositions for his lessons and designed my lessons. And he himself was never able to perfect this raag, and what I heard last night — it was perfect!" Sita replied, sounding a little bit relaxed.

Raavan's presence had startled her; her confidence took a dip whenever he was around. And when he wasn't, she was a different person. It had been many months, and she had become used to sitting amidst the giant ancient Ashok trees around her. Back in Janakpur and Ayodhya, Ashok trees were rare, which further alienated her initially as she limited herself to just one small area of the massive royal garden. The experience would at least have been a little more tolerable if the majority of trees were the familiar neems, apple, or guava trees, which were staples of the northern part of India, across the ocean. However, those Ashoks weren't alone. Surrounded by greenery, in the Ashok Vatika, there were banana trees, mangoes, palm trees, and whatnot, which she discovered much later. The garden was huge, and she had only seen half of it during her stay. She would sit still most of the day, shedding tears, and would roam around freely in the evenings, hum a few songs, play with the birds, drink fresh water from the lake in the middle of the garden, and bathe there as well. But the nights were unpleasant; she would miss Ram so much that she would enter a trance and lose herself. She enjoyed that daze momentarily, but in the end, once she came back to her senses, it would all end with the feeling of longing and utter despair, and she would regret it later.

Suman had become great, and the only companion to her. With nothing else to do, they would talk all day, about all things. Suman would tell her about how life was in Lanka. And much to Sita's surprise, it was actually quite good. There was no caste system; everybody lived peacefully, all happy in each other's company. Although there was no diversification of caste, there was a bit when it came to looks. Suman, the demon slave Raavan had assigned as the head caretaker of Sita, filled her in on things about Lanka.

"Sometimes, they grow tiny horns on their heads or their hair would never grow. They are the ones who eventually turn cannibals, so they have to be destroyed as soon as they are born, as per Raavan's orders. These birth deformities result from incest that is being practised behind closed doors in Lanka. Almost every household has one couple that is blood-related to each other. In order to maintain the sanctity of the bloodline of the family, they don't want to contaminate it by consummation with a person from another bloodline. These birth deformities are repercussions of this. Raavan has legalised incest, and this all happens under the law, but childbirth from such relationships is frowned upon and illegal. The royal decree states that there need be no bounds to whom we choose to love and how much. Some men make love to men, women do the same, and transgender people have a respectful standing in society. But he is strict about the purity of the population of Lanka. The weaker infants are either killed or donated to the army. I have heard of your world, where most people who are different from most are reduced to mere trolls and are decimated based on their sex. And people in your cities are materialists, with no taste for art. Under Raavan's rule, the art and music scene is always flourishing. Raavan himself looks after the Kala-Kendra that he has established in the outskirts of the city where students from all over the world come to study fine arts. Raavan himself is blessed with an impeccable artistic instinct and yet……" Suman realised midway that it was too much she was giving away. Sita, to her surprise, was keenly listening to her.

"Tell me, Suman, if everything you are telling me is true, then why is Raavan hated?" Sita spoke after a long pause.

"Not hated. Feared."

"Same thing."

"Different. You see, people over here believe that Raavan's might is perhaps too heavy a burden for the world to bear. He has literally worked on himself to make himself akin to a God in all aspects. His mortality is taken away from him. He is one of the few rare living beings on the planet, whom the true Gods blessed this way. However, he has his own vices. He can be a little arrogant and egoistic at times, but not always. Not to say he is a false God because he has these very human vices, but then a person who has largely risen above the human urges of lust, power, hunger, and mortality, can't be a mere mortal. You are one person who turned him into a human again, filled with lust, hunger for something besides enlightenment. And besides, how do you even define God in your lands? Worshipped and feared at the same time, and immortal? For a being, who is half demon and half Brahmin, this feat is beyond beliefs and that is why the world is afraid of him and stays away as far as they can." Suman sighed. Observing the glint in her eyes, Sita knew what was obvious; Suman had been in love with Raavan.

"Suman, I should be the last person to point that out, but over time, I have sensed some abstract sense of goodness in him. I don't know what feeling is this, but the aura that you are talking about is there in him. I saw him back then, I remember his face when he abducted me forcibly. I hated him though, I can't remember hating anything or anyone with the same burning passion that I did Raavan, but somewhere deep down I saw compassion, loneliness, hunger, pain. What happened to his sister was unfortunate but how was it Ram or Laxman's fault and most importantly, my fault…"

"But Sita, it was his sister after all." Suman said. "I must tell you one thing at this point in time. You don't know that he is a master in hypnotism, and I have seen him practising it on many of his enemies. And hence, he could make you his anytime he wants. He bears such forces under his thumb that he could kill your husband right away in an instant, wherever he is, in any corner of this universe. I have seen this happening with my own eyes. And yet, he wants to win you over, not because of your beautiful body or anything, but because he has probably fallen for the courage you possess. I have seen him over the years rejecting women, please don't mind, way more beautiful than you. I have known him for decades, and I can swear on the fact that he is not what you make out of him. He has compassion for everyone, even his enemies, and that compassion has stopped him from killing your husband right away or using the ethereal forces at his disposal to make you his. Now, I know what Ram means to you, and I completely sympathise with you. But the way you look at Raavan is something I don't particularly understand about you. You must respect him, Sita, at least. He is just a man in love and beings don't get any more irrational and fierce than a man in love."

"But you just said he was a God," Sita frowned.

"He was. Until you came into his life. You made him a man again. You came and woke his humane instincts, which I fear will be his doom. And I don't like the thought." Suman said, got up, and walked away to collect fruits.

Sita's brain stopped working; she heard everything and needed more time to process that much information. Those stories told to her about the Lanka kingdom that lay deep south beyond the ocean were completely opposite if Suman's words were to be believed. She had a clear glimpse of the golden city from above while she was being transported here, so she had very few doubts over Suman's claims. Since Sita was a kid, she was told that the city of Lanka was pure filth, full of cannibalism, disrespect, and a godless land. But as it came to be, it was anything but that. Raavan indeed was a great ruler, perhaps the greatest she had known. Lifting himself from the dirt and struggling through the naysayers for his existence as a half-brahmana-half-rakshasa, he embraced all the hate the world awarded him, made himself strong, and arose among all the men and the Gods to become the most learned and powerful being in the universe.

Suman had gone to sleep, and so had the sun. During the full moon night, Sita embarked on a slow walk through the beautiful garden, inhaling the fresh air through her nostrils deep into her lungs and exhaling it slowly through her mouth. And then she remembered the last 13 years that she had spent in constant insecurity, where life-threatening dangers lurked in every corner, with the man whom she married, Ram. In the Ashok Vatika, however, she had come across weird-looking snakes, lizards, and reptiles of all kinds, but she never felt threatened. As if those creatures were clearly instructed not to inflict any harm upon her. They stared at her, bowed their heads, and went about their ways digging holes and crawling on the tree trunks to feast on the bird-nests.

One creature, in particular, a giant snake with 12 bobbing heads, grabbed her attention like nothing else. The giant snake had only appeared once during the time that she had been here. She was pretty sure it was the guard snake that Raavan had employed to safeguard her from other venomous creatures. She knew it because once when that snake appeared, all the other creatures scattered away in any place they could find in the vast garden, just like a band of ants disperses at the first sight of danger. The snake never appeared again, but it strangely calmed her down, knowing all well that no harm would ever come to her as long as she was here.

She got tired of walking and sat down below a huge old mango tree, and looked at the moon which was only slightly visible among the dense leaves. She closed her eyes and almost slept when suddenly, she heard a rustling sound. She had become accustomed to the sounds of birds making the leaves rustle now and then, but this time, the sound had a different ambiance to it. She looked up. No doubt its source was the top of the tree, which she couldn't see amidst the dense leaves. After a few more seconds, the din swelled, and the branches of the tree started rustling, first slowly, then violently.

It was a monkey; she couldn't be mistaken. In the balmy moonlight, her vision caught the quick glimpse of a tail, thick and hairy, and then it became apparent that the tail was connected to a monkey, a monkey that was too enormous to be a monkey. It was big, almost as big as a human, perhaps even bigger. And before she knew it, with a loud thud, the monkey jumped right in front of her, sending a chill of horror through her spine. She almost fainted with fear. She was speechless and wanted to scream, but her voice box was paralyzed; she dragged herself back a couple of steps and pushed her back against the rough bark of the gigantic tree. What the monkey did next was totally inexplicable and surreal. He joined his palms, bowed down and spoke in a low baritone, "Maa." Mother.

'Is it one of those tricks Raavan has pulled off? No, Raavan can't possibly stoop this low. It must be a dream. This can be the only explanation.'

Sita shut her eyes tight and opened them again. The monkey was still there, bowed down, eyes closed, with palms still joined.

"Maate," the monkey whispered again, and it was the last thing she heard before she lost consciousness.

Raavan, sitting on the throne, surrounded by all his well-wishers and councilmen, was deep in thought while the hall was in chatter.

"Oh my King Dashaanan, does this really suit you? Maligning the name of the entire Rakshasa dynasty and over what? A measly human girl?" screamed one of the top-ranked councilmen with a shaking voice. He was about 90 years old and most of his face was drooping. He had to put in a hell lot of effort to get his voice heard for he was weak and was standing on his feet with the help of his middle-aged grandsons. "We have decided that this must be stopped at this very instant and return the woman back where she belongs. The word is spreading and you have become a laughingstock. Even if she agrees to be your wife, bringing her into your bed and palace would be a disgrace for us all."

Silence. Most of the councilmen were silently agreeing; this much was clear, but none dared to speak up or even nod.

Raavan scrutinized the old man calmly and kept staring at him until he lowered his eyes. Nobody questioned Raavan's authority, and that was one unspoken rule everyone followed. But this wasn't about it anymore; the topic remained unaddressed for close to a year now, and now it was the question of upholding the respect the royal crown of Lanka had earned through decades of work, countless battles and victories, which was worth facing Raavan's wrath for. People were talking, and rumours were spreading about Raavan's weakness. Falling in one-sided love didn't suit the stature of a King who was universally feared.

"I respect you all. I am at your service and the city's till the day I die, but about that woman, Sita, that is a personal matter, I say. I concur that I lost my privacy privileges the day I sat on the throne of this great kingdom. But again, I don't ask what all of you do in your own houses behind closed doors, and to be more forward, I could care less. So try that, leave me alone and let me handle her my own way. And also, henceforth, no one talks disrespectfully of her," Raavan said the last part with enhanced emphasis and a voice that roared down through the entire council hall.

"But you know what rumours have been floating around. Her husband, who is supposed to be a warrior prince from far north across the ocean, is on the hunt and soon, he will reach the shores. This is war!" exclaimed one other councilman, fairly young, almost the same age as Raavan.

"So, we should be scared?" another young gun spoke out in fury. It was Raavan's son, Indrajeet. "Should we forget what those vile humans did to my beloved aunt Shoorpanakha? And more importantly, should we be scared of two men with no army?"

"That's not the point; God knows there is no force in the entire universe that could match ours. But unnecessary bloodshed over a human who couldn't even match the beauty of the great queen — that is idiotic, utterly idiotic. And I guess you are too busy with your mistresses that you haven't heard that Baali's younger brother King Sugreev has joined hands with Ram," said the old Raakshasa, sitting down.

"As my father said, it's not about the woman, as much as it's about sending a message. Whatever our king desires, he shall have. Let's fight and ascertain the fact once again: Raavan is the…."

The young lad became silent as Raavan rose from his golden throne. He ambled straight out of the hall, leaving the councilmen all bewildered and astonished.

It turned out even the best of them are as corruptible as the commoners. The wisest and the bravest man there ever was was corrupted, and his corruption was love.

When Sita regained her consciousness, she still found the talking monkey sitting in front of her. But for some reason, she was less anxious now. She carefully observed him. At a height of six feet, he was at least a foot taller than Sita and well built, with strong arms, veins protruding out of them, covered with inch-long white hair. He wore nothing but a loincloth, and his creamy white fur made Sita wonder how he managed to camouflage himself and arrive at the Ashok Vatika untouched and unscathed.

And then the monkey began talking, and he seemed pretty talkative, for he went on non-stop for almost half an hour. With each passing minute, the wise monkey had managed to strip away all apprehensions Sita had regarding him. This strange monkey claimed to be Ram's loyal squire and was here to take Sita away from the clutches of the demon King.

"Mother, I have seen men in love, and then I have seen Ram. There is no love greater than his for you. I have dedicated my entire life to the service of your husband, and nothing would make him happier than bringing you back to him, and in turn, I would think my life's goal is fulfilled," he pleaded. "I could destroy this entire city in minutes if you allow me to, or I could sweep you out of here right at this moment, and no one would ever know what happened here."

By now Sita was accustomed to long silences. She just smiled at him as the monkey named Hanuman stood there impatiently. It was just a matter of time before anybody would notice him, and then it would become improbable to harmlessly carry Sita to Ram. And patience isn't the virtue monkeys possess.

"I thank you from the bottom of my heart, my dear. But this can't be done, pardon me. Why didn't he come himself, if he loved me that much, I ask?"

Hanuman gathered all his wits but couldn't come up with an appropriate response to that. Or he was awestruck by the aura of Sita's beautiful voice so much that his tongue jammed.

"Please go and tell my love that I am quite safe here. And I am waiting for him to rescue me, personally."

Hanuman's mind worked even faster, and he already started thinking of the excuses he needed to make to Ram as to why he didn't bring Sita back if he had located her whereabouts. Hanuman joined his palms and knelt, touching her feet through his forehead. "Tathastu," she blessed him. Hanuman knew that there was not an ounce of doubt over what Sita wanted. She was determined, so any further attempt to bring her back would be futile.

Hanuman stood up and warmed up a bit before the big flight, stretched his body, and turned his gaze towards the lush greenery around. There were ripe mangoes, bananas, apples, pomegranates, and whatnot hanging from the trees. That was when his monkey instincts stirred up, and he looked at Sita with a hint of guilt. She understood what was going on; the saliva accumulated on the left corner of his mouth said it all.

"You are hungry?" she asked, smiling.

"Indeed. Constantly shifting shapes and sizes and flying consumes energy, and the love I bear in my heart for your lord husband only feeds me so much. With your permission, I would like to just…" he bowed his head down.

"Do as you please, my dear." She smiled. She looked around; Suman was nowhere to be seen. Sita would have loved to introduce them to each other.

Hanuman got the licence and what he did next snatched away all the impressions of being just a 'regular monkey who happens to look like a man' from him, in a matter of a few seconds. His divine self activated, and he shifted shapes and sizes frequently and hopped from tree to tree to gobble the fruits. And once he was done with eating, it was just reckless chaos in the gorgeous garden. His wise words contrasted strongly with what Sita was witnessing. He pulled out the trees from their roots, threw them around, used them to smash other trees, and roared, making sure that the noise was heard in all of Lanka. And needless to say, it got the attention of all the guards appointed in the palace adjacent to the garden. They all came charging towards him, and by now he was almost 15 feet in size. With enormous strength, he destroyed all the guards in a matter of seconds. He pulled out their limbs, threw trees at them, and sent their dismembered bodies across the tall walls of the garden. There were about 15 of them, and some of them were killed right there while a few saved themselves and ran back into the dining hall, straight to Raavan.

For a second, Raavan couldn't believe what was being told to him.

A monkey, a damned monkey.

Raavan stood up and ordered his older son Meghanad, sitting and eating at the same table, who was called Indrajeet (conqueror of Indra) for a reason, to take care of the business.

Meghanad rushed to the garden, captured Hanuman in a matter of moments, and brought him back to Raavan, who had moved to the court by then, and walked away to conclude his meal.

A cocky warrior Meghanad was, although one of the best.

Raavan held a council meeting right away, and when Hanuman told him that doom was upon him, as Ram was already on his way to Lanka, a commotion erupted in the hall between the shouts to kill him right then and there. Raavan didn't have much say in the matter, but when he came to know that he intended to take Sita away, he became reckless and ordered death by fire. It was a sudden order; he quickly came back to his senses in no time and signalled the executioner to stop.

"I know you, Monkey Man; you are one of the loyal servants of Sugreev. His brother Baali was a good friend of mine, and I understand that your lord, that Ram, killed him deceivingly and handed over the kingdom of Kishkindha to his vile brother. Even though I wasn't much fond of him, Baali was a good man. We had some friendly duels a few times, and I could vouch for the fact that he was the only person in the entire world who could even come close to beating me in a hand-to-hand duel. That said, Ram seems to be a coward to me. He sends you here, knowing all well that it means certain death; why didn't he come himself? What are you if not a bait…"

"Speak with caution, Raavan; this might be the last time anybody from our side is standing in front of you without the motive of tearing you apart to shreds. You have no idea who you are talking about."

Raavan was amused. The arrogance was taking over.

A petty monkey challenging Raavan's might.

Oh Sita, the things I have to endure for you.

Raavan rubbed his palms, grinding his teeth, and stood up.

"Burn the monkey," he declared and left.

He walked out of the hall again, in the middle of the meeting, as Hanuman's eyes followed him. The monkey was tied with a mystic rope, which even the strongest of Gods couldn't untie. Right there, right in the middle of the hall, the fire was set on his tail. Raavan, before walking out of the door, turned and got a good glimpse at him, knowing all too well that this was no ordinary monkey, and he wouldn't die this easily. He walked toward Ashok Vatika to understand the 'damage'.

Ignoring the mutilated bodies of his guards and stomping over them, he rushed straight to Sita to have a heart-to-heart with her, which he knew was one of his final attempts. This time around, the way he walked was different. The walk was infiltrated with a sharp rage and fury.

"You don't understand. You just won't understand. And now I give up on you. This right here, remember this moment, when I say that I am letting you go, from my heart, not from Ashok Vatika," he said. "Let him come, let me judge him all by myself. Let the great Ram walk into my Lanka. I think the monkey has already filled you in about how they have gathered an army full of wild beasts to tackle me," he scoffed.

Sita stood up.

"The end is near, your end," Sita said with pride, conviction, and fury. Hanuman's arrival had infused a sense of accomplishment and confidence in her voice. She looked up at the sky, towards the west, where Raavan held his court and smiled. The flames were kissing the skies; people were crying in pain. There was no doubt over what had transpired. Sita darted at Raavan with a victorious grin. Raavan's eyes were unmoved. His gaze was locked on Sita's beautiful eyes.

It was no surprise to Raavan, as if he envisioned the damage being done to his beloved capital. As if he knew what was going to happen the moment he asked to have Hanuman burned alive. His gaze didn't waver. Sita's fluttering eyes were the only sight that mattered for him at that moment. Way more than his beloved golden city, which was returning to ashes from where it was built.

They both stood there, eye to eye. Raavan was almost a foot taller than her, and she had to move her neck upwards to cut a deep stare into his bloodshot eyes. There was nothing but agony in them. An ocean was building up in his eyes; Sita could see it. Raavan was finally breaking. Sita knew it, and in that very instant, she understood one thing quite clearly.

'Standing in front of me, the mighty Raavan, the king of Lanka, the conqueror of Gods, the one with ten heads and twenty arms, the most powerful man in the world. And his heart is shattered into pieces. He, in fact, is maddeningly, explicitly in love with me. He won't even touch me! He will do me no harm.'

As if reading her thoughts, Raavan turned back and left, wiping away the tears.

He walked straight into the half-burnt council hall and walked over to the grand throne, which was reduced to half-burnt iron and molten gold. The rest of the council hall was still standing, with all the councilmen practically unmoved. They needed orders. Raavan looked up at the massive hole in the golden roof of the hall; above it, he could not see the moon or the stars, for a dense dark cloud of ashes had blocked his sight. He had built this ceiling with great care and loved it a lot. The anguish and pain in his heart had no boundaries.

The confused chatter among the councilmen was replaced by a stunned silence. Raavan's red eyes said it all; it was the time.

His voice echoed once again down the hall as he finally proclaimed, "War!"

He sat on whatever was left of the throne as the crowd slowly dispersed, and he was left all alone in the throne room.

The gods above rejoiced.

The demons of Lanka awaited their doom.

Across the ocean, Ram sighed, looking at the cloud of smoke, shut his eyes and whispered — "Sita."
