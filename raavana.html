Raavana

By Manoj Ashodia · Lanka, the Golden City

Pouring out all his might and creativity on the gigantic veena resting on his lap, the Demon King drove the entire palace numb and silent as if the world had come to a halt, basking in the glory of the one true musical God at his terrifying best. Nobody moved an inch, and not because they were following a protocol. No one wanted to abandon the chance to relish this exquisite sound emanating out of the golden palace standing tall right into the centre of the golden city of Lanka. The purple walls of the music room were supposed to be soundproof, as the engineers had declared, but then, they had never heard the Lankesh performing music.

With his mountainous physique, he was seated on a velvet mat with the enormous instrument on his lap, and his fingers moved restlessly on the strings, bleeding, creating the kind of heavenly music only the one truly blessed by Saraswati could. This utter passion for melody either results from extreme pain or extreme joy, as any real musician can testify.

While she herself was hypnotized by it, Mandodari, his beautiful wife, lying in her bedroom a few paces away waiting for him, knew what it was all about. The pain she was feeling, of unrequited love, resonated with the pain in her husband's heart, channelling through a veena as if Raavan understood her heartache and was striving to express it through the music. But then she knew that both their aches told two different stories and arose from two very different feelings. She felt it for her husband, and he, for the lonely another man's woman sitting in Ashok Vatika.

It was the first time in months that he had been to the music room alone and that too deep into the night. Usually, he entered the room with 10–12 of his closest art patrons from around the empire, to exhibit his skills and learn new things; that was if they, in fact, could teach him anything. When it came to music, Raavan was no narcissist.

This late-night session meant that something else was bothering him as well, something huge, something not beyond Mandodari's unmaligned sensibilities could conjure up. Her innocent mind of a love-hungry wife wished that the remorse he was feeling was due to his inability to love Mandodari as she deserved. Or perhaps, his agony was triggered by the realization that he had caused immense heartache to Ram, the abducted woman's husband. Or perhaps it was a brutal combination of the three, rendering the greatest King in all three worlds sleepless.

Mandodari knew her husband, and she was aware that in all the years, she had not seen him being unjust, and perhaps the thoughts of his recent erratic actions were eating him up.

She sighed and kept her gaze locked on the golden door of the bedroom, waiting for her husband to emerge from the gigantic door. As much as she wanted the angelic music to resume, she was aching to hear the thumping footsteps of her beloved husband approaching her more than anything else.

… who was dying to see her. Besides, no woman, whether she be a human or a demon queen, can bear the pain of her husband ruining his sleep over some other woman. Mandodari was a woman strong of character and loved her husband more than anything and anyone else in the entire universe. Despite his characteristic lapses, she always knew there was a whole other side to her husband that was generally hidden from plain sight — crept inside silently, stripped of any desire to be measured by the very human perceptions of morality and immorality. Raavan was not a human; hence, the concept of principles was alien to him. Garbed in arrogance, his greatness was always doomed to be feared, not loved or respected.

Geniuses are more often than not difficult to understand, and abhorred by the majority. Outside this little Southern island of Lanka, Raavan was just a demon king who 'deserved' to be destroyed.

At dawn, he finally opened the shiny entrance of his sleep chamber and stood by his bed, staring at Mandodari. Faintly snoring, she had decided to shut her eyes for a moment but ended up launching herself into a deep sleep.
