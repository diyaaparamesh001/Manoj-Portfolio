Butterfly

By Manoj Ashodia

On a lazy summer afternoon of 2023, a butterfly flew into my garden and was crushed under the weight of my sorrows. In another world, the butterfly flapped its wings and contemplated what her fault was. She breathlessly waited for me to answer it for her. However, I could only write a very sad letter and hoped for it to reach her in time before she lost her faith in humanity.

Later, as I read it again and again, I found the letter to be sadder than that butterfly's fate, and I beat myself up over why I only write sad things. I burned the letter.

I wrote another one, and burned it too.

A day and a half later, my doorbell rang as the postman brought a letter from the butterfly. I read it in one breath. I found words stuck in my throat and thought the sad letter I had written had some substance that the butterfly would have liked. So I found some parts of the letter in the ashes right outside my house.

Those fragments went like…

'…there was a devil in my backyard that disapproved of the beauty you brought… and then I always wished I should die… my apologies would never suffice and they wouldn't be able to touch even a sliver of pain you suffered when the weight of my sorrows crushed you under them… your wings remind me of the sad childhood of an orphan who traveled to the end of the universe to find someone that could love him. and just like that he died, waiting… the orphan boy believed he was incapable of 'good love', so when a woman offered him love without conditions, he betrayed her thinking it was 'too good to be true'… you see, we are raised believing we are not deserving of the love that Rumi talked about in 'love is the bridge between you and everything'...there was another beggar on the street who stared directly into my eyes and begged for my tears because no one gave him theirs and he was short of them for himself. I gave him my lover.'

I pasted these fragments together and sent them to the butterfly. Strangely, she wasn't very pleased. She wrote a scathing letter saying I was too sad a man and she wouldn't want anything to do with me. I was shocked, and the devil on my back mocked.

I untied myself from the devil and regretted it immediately after. The devil asked me if I really do exist without him or if he is an integral part of me as a whole. I feared becoming unrecognizable. In the process, I also broke my left toe and walked like a cripple. I looked for the butterfly everywhere.

I failed in my endeavors. I looked everywhere and even traveled to the edge of the universe, but with no luck.

Decades later, after I returned, I found the devil still on my back. I asked it some not-so-difficult questions to think about, but yes, to ask they were nearly impossible.

Me- "I had left you behind in my previous life. What are you doing here?"

Devil- "I had left, but that butterfly asked me not to leave you alone. I wondered if you wanted to fulfill her wishes."

Me- "But I have love. I don't need you anymore."

Devil- "You have love, but you shouldn't forget what happens every time you fall in love. You crush a butterfly. The butterfly wants it to end."

Me- "Are you loyal to her more than me?"

Devil- "No. You love your sorrows more than your joys."

Me- "Not true."

Devil- "You all do."

The devil didn't vanish. I can't tell you how difficult it is to write with a devil sitting constantly on my back. But I guess you become so used to it that it almost seems to be invisible. As Albert Camus said- 'Given enough time, a person can get used to anything.'

"Let us go and see that butterfly…" I said.

"But…?"

"Anything said after a but makes the part before the 'but' useless. Let's not waste the words with your but."

"But I didn't say anything before the 'but'."

"Precisely."

And we set out on another expedition to an unknown land where the ghosts of the butterflies are said to be lurking around.
