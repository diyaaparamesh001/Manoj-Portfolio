Bhola: The Lovesick Ghost

By Manoj Ashodia · Mandavali Village

"So, you want to tell me that not every human likes to wear their feelings on their sleeves, some know how to hide them behind a curt smile? Is that what you want to tell me?" the ghost asked, perplexed, taking and losing cloudy shapes under the old banyan tree, talking to the head priest of the Hanuman temple that was built by King Kooderaam Singh on the outskirts of Mandavali Village.

It was dusk, and the ghost was feeling nauseous. Not that human deceit was an alien concept, he was just too naive to wrap his inconspicuous head around it.

"Kill me, Lord Krishna. Even for a ghost, you're tremendously stupid. For the last time, all I'm saying is that my daughter doesn't think it's in her best interest to marry a ghost," replied an irritated Vitthal Prasaad while rolling his eyes, followed by a scowl. "She likes you but she doesn't think it's right to marry a ghost. So, it's a no from her side," the priest added.

In the calm of the night, a curious owl sat like a guard on the lowest branch of the old Banyan tree, staring down at the angriest priest in the town who was trying to convince a lovesick ghost that alive-and-well girls only liked ghosts because unlike men, they are pretty much unbothered about worldly attachments.

"So please, listen to me Bhola, you are as good a ghost as you can be but you are too naïve if you think a human would ever choose to marry you. So, yeah. Let's forget it and never discuss it again," the priest said conclusively, preparing to leave after he was done with his ritualistic conversation with the ghost of a former royal toilet-cleaner named Bhola Bhandari.

He WAS naive, for believing that dying would relieve him of the bane of living as a lower caste man.

"You owe me one, you priest. It was only because of you that I chose to turn into a ghost because you promised me your daughter's hand in marriage once I die," the ghost said, with a stab wound in his back as deep as his love for a high-caste girl who had caught and kept Bhola's affection ever since they first met when they were in their early teens and attacked each other's mouths with their tongues.

The priest scoffed and in his familiar angry gait, he strode back to the Hanuman temple. The ghost could only stare at this vile man helplessly, who had crushed his hope of ever being in the arms of the person he loved the most, the ugliest girl in the town, Roopa Prasaad.

"What happened," asked the owl in his deep wise baritone. Bhola assumed the shape of a bat and sat beside the owl on the lowest hanging branch of the old Banyan tree. "I guess I have been betrayed. Roopa told her father that she would marry me if I killed myself and turned into a ghost. Now, she had backtracked on her promise and I am dead. You can ask Shantanu," said Bhola the Bat tapping his right foot on the branch.

In a hoarse sleepy voice, the tree spoke, "Vilachhan, how many times have I told you I don't entertain Bats anymore. Shoo away!"

The Owl laughed out loud. "Shantanu ji, its Bhola messing around in a bat's body, believing a human's promises are worth anything," he said while giggling like a hyena with a fractured lower jaw.

The old Banyan tree laughed out loud, and its leaves rattled like a pot filled with gold coins King Kooderam had promised the priest if he just ensured his daughter would never ever go near Bhola again.

"Again? Bhola, I'd give you that. You're quite persistent," groaned the Banyan tree followed by a short chuckle that shook only a few leaves this time.

"Hear hear. As if the old grump has anything better to do but to stand here all day entertaining the priest whom he badmouths in the nights," the Ghost bitterly attempted to avenge the mockery.

"You three, would you just stop your rambling. The ugly girl Roopa is approaching," Bansi the snake chimed in.

"Oh, Bansi, scram. Don't scare her off like the last time," Bhola the bat panicked as he saw the love of his life walking towards him.

"Bhola, you there?" Roopa said in a low-pitched voice. She looked up at the owl and the bat. Confunsingly, she asked, "now which one's you then?"

Bhola the Bat undid his shape and blew himself down in a puff of smoke. "For God's sake, stop showing off and take a shape," Roopa was low on patience.

Bhola's ghost took the shape of a rabbit and stood on the ground on his rear legs, gawking at Roopa's hideous face as he did when he first laid his eyes on her all those years back while cleaning the massive shit in King's bathroom as a teenager.

"Please don't be mad at me," pleaded Roopa. Bhola just knew something bad had happened that she didn't attempt a funny jibe at his rabbit form. So preparing himself for the bad news, he asked "what?"

"You remember Sundar?"

"Yeah, the mason's younger son who drowned in the pond a month ago," Bhola said curiously.

"Yeah, I'm marrying him in a fortnight," Roopa said, staring down at her feet.

"That snitch, who doesn't even know the basics of ghost-ethics," murmured an angry Bhola.

"So, I just want to let you know that you should forget me and find yourself a suitable… ghost lady," Roopa said rather confidently.

"But he doesn't even know how to clean a shithole properly Roopa."

Roopa dug her right fingernails into her skin and dragged out the feelings and tied them on her sleeve.

"I didn't come here to argue, Bhola," Roopa said and left.

"That scoundrel Pushpak.. I tell you he has a hand in this," Shantanu said grinding his teeth that didn't exist.

"Whatever, Shantanu Ji. I don't think it's the right time…Wait.. Where's Bhola," the Owl bobbed his head in all directions.

Bhola the Ghost, a lovesick immortal had been consumed by extreme grief.

"Hi grief," said Bansi.

"Hi guys. Good to be back," said grief and burped as he had just consumed the 4524th lovesick ghost.
