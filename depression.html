Mental Health: Depression

By Manoj Ashodia · A Ghost and a Human

"Hey Human. Are you there?"

"Yes, Ghost. Tell me, what now?"

"What is depression?"

"I am not sure?"

"What do you mean? You are a human and aren't you guys pretty accustomed to feeling depress..?"

"I know the feeling. But I don't know if that's the right word to express that feeling when you are…"

"What? Depressed"

"Would you stop using this word? It is hurting my brain?"

"Okay. But what exactly is the feeling of being… you know I am talking about?"

"Its not that easy to describe either. It works differently for everyone."

"How does it work for you?"

"Are we really going to get into this discussion? You are a ghost. Why the hell would you care about us humans and all sorts of overly dramatic terms we conjure up to make our existence spicier?"

"Okay. Is it that hard to describe?"

"No, okay. Look at it this way. It's a feeling I wouldn't even want my worst enemy to have, you getting me?"

"Ummm… Does it feel good when you're in that feeling?"

"No. The opposite."

"Then why wouldn't you wish that on your worst enemy. Don't you want to hurt them as badly as you can?"

"Precisely. Now, imagine worse than the thing that I can to them that would hurt them to extremes. That feeling that we're talking about is worse than that. It's the scariest feeling in the pool of feelings that we are meant to feel…"

"Okay. That's interesting. Did you ever get it?"

"Yes. Often."

"You never told me."

"It's only been a year since I have known you. And its not something you often talk about. Even talking about it is nerve-inducingly scary and terrifying."

"I love nerve inducingly terrifying and scary things. I am a ghost."

"Oh. But this one is a real mess."

"Try me, you weak-hearted human."

"Okay. It feels like your life gets divided in two halves- before 'it' happens and after 'it' happens. When you are into that feeling, you feel like you're sleepwalking through your day. All the familiar places become unfamiliar and you become a stranger in even your own house. As if you are not welcome there anymore. But you have nowhere else to go. So, you are stuck and you feel that all the time, of not belonging anywhere and all alone, stranded on a dark scary road at night with several scary demons lurking at you, ready to pounce at you at the first chance. You understand? It is like the feeling you feel when you are stuck in a lift a thousand feet above the floor, without any source of ventilation, without any sound around you, and without a door or a window. Your chest is heavier as if the oxygen supply to your lungs have been cut off. You feel that there is no escaping that dread and there is nothing to look forward to and nothing to look back to. All the pleasant memories of your past are blacked out and what remains is a crippling sense of regret, self-pity, as if your life always has been nothing but a long insufferable epoch full of darkness and you wonder how you have survived until now. So in a way, it's the most beautiful feeling in the world, and the worst feeling at the same time."

"Goddamn. What is good about that feeling?"

"The good part is what comes later. When you come out of the feeling. And its as easy to slip out of it than slipping inside it, at-least for some people. For me, it works in that way. Think of a man in a Siberian prison, who has not seen food, water or sunlight in days. He has been imprisoned for life. What do you think happens to the prisoner? Why doesn't he take his own life when there is no way out of his cell?"

"I don't know."

"Hope. Even a small golden ray of sunlight is enough to give him immeasurable hope which effectively makes him go on and sooner or later, there comes a day, when the prisoner is released as the new Russian government altered some life-imprisonment laws. And the life that begins beyond that experience is what makes that prisoner a good man, a man who understands suffering and who knows the value of freedom. Empathy! That experience, quite vaguely, fills the man with appreciation for even the smallest things. He will cherish it, make the best out of it and ensure that the remainder of his life isn't wasted. You know, suffering shapes us in many ways so effectively that we can't comprehend. It has done a lot for me; it can do a lot for others. In-fact, everyone should go through that feeling of utter hopelessness once in a while, just to experience the other side of it, that is better than anything in the world. A hungry man is served a meal after days of extreme famine. A fat man is offered the same meal for lunch after having a full breakfast. Who will appreciate the meal more? For whom will that meal be the most special thing in the world and fill him with glee and happiness beyond any measure?"

"The one who was hungry."

"Yes. Its as simple as that, Ghost."

"Okay. Human."

"But why wouldn't you want this feeling for even your worst enemies though? It doesn't sound as bad."

"Because I am not into that feeling right now. Its easy to look back in retrospect and then it doesn't sound as bad. But when you're into that feeling, you wish for death. You are driving on the road and you wish a truck hits you and kills you on the spot. You pray for a heart attack or a brain haemorrhage. Just because you aren't brave enough to kill yourself. The person thinks of their loved ones and how regretful they would feel, blaming themselves for not paying enough attention and whatnot. And that feeling, only intensifies the dread of that feeling that you feel. So, it's a mad rabbit-hole of sorts."

"Well, why doesn't the love of your loved ones work like an anchor to pull yourself out of that feeling? Don't you think that being in that state, impacts your loved ones as well? If you truly love the people around you, don't you feel a responsibility to put in more efforts to make yourself better?"

"Because, the despair of your existence significantly dwarves the love you have for your loved ones."

"Its quite complicated, it seems."

"Yes. It is."

"Okay. What caused you to feel that way, if you are willing to share?"

"Something that feels like a distant nightmare now. I was in love."

"Here we go again. You stupid beings, gloating over your intelligence above all other species, yearning for another human's love? Who was she?"

"She was someone with whom I felt… different and more in touch with myself than any other person I had met prior to her and have met since she left."

"Okay. But a million people go through this feeling on a daily basis. It's not like you are alone in the world to whom the worse is happening. I think you all know that."

"You see. Sometimes, actually, most times, you never know what would trigger that feeling? There are many people who can't even pinpoint the exact cause. That feeling just comes uninvited."

"Damn. So it can come to you even right now, when you seemingly look fine, well and healthy to me?"

"Oh yes."

"Evolutionary psychology is to blame?"

"To some extent yeah. Its only been what, less than 20,000 years, when we were busy all day, hunting and killing and populating the earth. The modern human has ample idle time and what does a human do when they have nothing to think or do?"

"They strive to find meaning in everything?"

"Yes, precisely. There was a time before the birth of Christ, not too long ago, when the civilizations had finally come to a point when there was nothing left to do. Food was sorted, houses and villages were raised. Across the civilizations, information was being exchanged as new ways of making lives easier and more comfortable was travelling like wildfire. And in that scenario, the age of thinkers began, most famously in Greece, when people had ample time to think about the meaning."

"The meaning of what?"

"Everything. Why the sun set, why was the land the way it was, what did it mean to be humans and some interesting ideas cropped up amidst those musings of elderly thinkers. But somewhere from there, was produced the thought that life was inherently meaningless and we are just doomed to pass through our existence, towards our set demise and we could do nothing to alter that. The modern human is facing more-or-less the same existential dilemma. There are not many things to do with the advent of technology and enough idle time is a slow poison that is being injected into the masses on a huge scale. And then, there are people who have zero idle time. They feel the same. The true peace lies somewhere in between and striking that balance is what causes many people to feel that feeling."

"Wow. There are many aspects to it. But what's the solution?"

"Why does there need to be a solution to each problem. Some problems are good as problems. They keep us busy and hence, sometimes, it causes these feelings to elude us. Which is good right?"

"But sometimes, problems cause such feelings?"

"Yes. Human life is complicated."

"Oh. I agree."

"Oh yes. You're a ghost. How would you know."

"I am better off not knowing."

"Okay. I will go back to sleep now."

"Sleep in peace. Good Night, Human."

"Good Night, Ghost."
