A Fictional Battle: Egyptians vs Spartans

By Manoj Ashodia · Ancient Thebes

King Pleistarcus's Spartan horde marched eastward toward the Nile. The Spartans weren't reputed to go to war in quiet. As they crossed through the seas, their roars thundered through the skies and reached the pharaoh Queen Cleopatra X's gorgeously shaped ears, who immediately ordered her army to prepare for the war. Nobody in the Egyptian council had seen the Spartans fight but the lores and distant foreigner songs earned them a reputation that they 'fought like the Gods among men'.

Finally, the doomsday arrived and the armies met at Thebes, 100 miles from the Nile, the lifeline of the entire Egypt. For the God-fearing Egyptians, it was easy to throw away the weapons and bow down to the great Spartan, King Pleistarcus, the heroics of whose father King Leonidas was well-known across the lands and seas.

The Spartan Army was remarkably larger, with at least 50,000 infantrymen, and 90,000 horsemen rallying behind. Egyptians, on the other hand, had just suffered the most terrifying famine, which couldn't have come at a wronger time. The wrath of God Horus had left the Egyptian army with only about 30,000 healthy soldiers. Things looked bleak for them but even then, there was no fear on their faces. Their heads were straight with pride, and their chests swelled. The Egyptians were prepared to die, perhaps.

Spartans charged with all their might, led by their brave king Pleistarcus on a white horse, chanting a foreign war cry that made the land and the skies shudder. The sky was covered in dirt. The Egyptians waited with bated breaths. Prayers could be heard among the front-line infantry. Soon enough, the land below their feet was painted red with thick red Egyptian blood. It seemed like the Spartans had been hungry for some foreign blood and it was the time to have their fill.

The Spartans were known for their war tactics. Owing to the Agoge regimen, every single Spartan soldier fought like a trained general. But here, their training was of no use because they were facing the very primal Egyptian army in which only one among 3 was properly trained for battle. Even their weapons were outdated. For Spartans, it seemed like a walk in the park, as they sliced the Egyptians like they were moving knives through a slice of butter.

But something seemed off.

The Egyptians were giving in too easily. Was that tactic? The Spartans were blunt, and brave, but lacked a certain intellect. They were trained in battles, not the art of war.

Cleopatra smirked, sitting in her large golden hall several miles away, and whispered something intangible.

And then suddenly, screeching sounds pierced through the battlefield, suppressing the Spartan howling, and in a flash, the entire Egyptian army vanished in the dust.

It was a mirage, a miracle performed by the Godly queen Cleopatra who was well-versed in the dark arts. The Spartans were left aghast. They weren't used to such sorcery on the battlefield.

King Pleistarcus, who had bathed in fierce Egyptian blood, felt his heart sinking, anticipating the worst.
